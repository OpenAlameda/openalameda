## Methodology & disclosures

### How the site is built

OpenAlameda is built with agentic development tools (AI coding agents). A person reviews every change through an ordinary pull request before it ships.

### No AI at runtime

The site itself uses no AI. Every figure comes from a deterministic query over public data.

### Data sources

Campaign finance data comes from the <a href={SOURCE_URL}>City of Alameda campaign disclosure portal (Granicus CampaignDocs)</a>.

### What is counted and what is not

Everything on the election pages comes from the campaign disclosure filings candidates and committees send the City. Filing collection is supervised, directed by a person and done at a pace which is respectful of the resources of the City's portal. Because of that, the numbers are only as current as the date shown on each page ("data through"), and they do not update on their own.

The figures cover itemized contributions only. A campaign is required to provide information about a donor's gifts once they total $100 or more in a campaign period. Smaller gifts are reported only as a lump sum, so they are not in the donor breakdowns. Each card says how much was raised and spent in all, and the grids and percentages describe the itemized money from others.

### Amendments, late reports and loans

When a campaign amends a report, we use the newest version and ignore the one it replaced. Reports filed on paper are transcribed, and each is marked "from paper filing." For those, we compare the summary page with the schedules behind it. If they disagree, the card uses the schedules and we note the difference ("reported vs. schedule"). The "loans received" line repeats from one report to the next, so we only note changes there.

A loan is not money raised. A loan the candidate made to their own campaign is shown on its own line, as "loaned by the candidate." If a candidate forgives a loan, the campaign's Form 460 would then report it as a contribution, so we count it once, as money from the candidate, and do not add it on top.

Late contribution reports (the 24-hour "497" reports) work the same way: the loans on them are counted as loans, not as money raised.

### Money from the candidate and carried over

Two kinds of money are in the "raised" total but kept out of the grids and percentages, so those show who else is paying: the candidate's own contributions, and money carried over from the candidate's earlier campaign committees. Each appears as its own line on the card.

### Where and who donations come from

We categorize donation sources geographically and by donor type.

Geographically, we divide donations into "Inside the city of Alameda," "Inside Alameda County," and "Everywhere Else." For donor type, we sort into "Individual", "Organization" for businesses, unions, or other groups; and "Political" for donations from other campaigns, political action committees (PACs), and political parties. All of this information comes directly from the campaign's filings.

A donor counts as "City of Alameda" if their ZIP code is 94501 or 94502. Any other ZIP code in Alameda County counts as "Alameda County." Everything else, and any gift with a missing or unreadable ZIP, is "Other." The county list comes from the Census Bureau's 2020 table that matches ZIP areas to counties. A ZIP code that crosses the county line is assigned to the county that holds the largest share of its land. Some ZIP codes, such as 94708 (Kensington and the Berkeley hills), are therefore not on the list.

When a paper filing is unclear, we record each reading decision, including whether an AI agent or a person made it and when, show it on the candidate's page, and keep the log in `data/elections/<election>/corrections.json`.

### Outside groups

Outside groups are committees that spend money to support or oppose a candidate or measure without being that campaign. We count their independent expenditures from the 24-hour "496" reports they file after their latest regular report. A group that files only the short Form 450 gets all of its 496 reports counted, because a Form 450 does not break those out. Outside spending that we could not tie to a race or candidate is not added to anyone's total, and the election page does not list it. Money that a committee gives directly to a campaign is in that campaign's raised total and is not outside spending.

### Names

We never show the name of an individual donor, only totals. Organizations and political committees may be named.

### Filings we couldn't read

Some handwritten filings can be hard to read. We seek to transcribe them faithfully, and record the decisions we make for transparency. In some extreme cases, we may not be able to reach a confident decision; that data is shown separately in an amber-highlighted note and is not included in any totals.
